As you can see from my CV, I have not taken a direct path to the field of anthropology.
While my bachelor’s  degree is in communication, I did not start my undergraduate studies as a communication major. I began as a biology major. I loved science and was fascinated by the natural world. After my first semester, however, I became overwhelmed with hard science. What I loved about biology was studying living things and the life seemed to disappear when taken down to the molecular and cellular level. I realized that what I was interested in was not so much tissues or cells but the people themselves, their way of life, and their culture. I began taking classes in media and communication to learn more about people and their cultures. While not precisely anthropology, my coursework has extensive overlap in social anthropology with many units covering theories of diversity, class, social identity, gender, ethics, cultural objects, sociology, visual culture, and film. Because of this deviating and indirect academic path, my undergraduate studies covered numerous disciplines and have provided me with both the quantitative and qualitative analytical skills necessary to study anthropology.
Similarly, my work experiences have not led me down a direct path. I have dabbled in many fields since graduating. I began my professional life with jobs in the visual arts, first working with a photographer for a year before working at a talent agency for several years. Then, I taught myself how to design and build websites and moved on to web design and web development. After doing that for a few years, I moved to Thailand to become an English teacher. This varied professional work has given me relevant experience for the study of anthropology as I have dealt with human culture at each of my jobs whether through creating visual images in photography, managing international talent at the talent agency, researching user experience in web design, or exchanging language and culture as an English instructor in Thailand. 
Over the past few months, I have had a some time to think about my life and I finally began connecting the dots of my meandering journey through my academic and professional life. I wondered, “What is it that motivates me to keep moving on to the next phase throughout my life?”It became increasingly apparent. It’s the same thing that forced me to change my major ten years ago: my fascination of people and their culture. I’m fascinated by the diversity of human life and my appreciation of this diversity is the thread that has run through each chapter of my life.
I grew up in a small, conservative rural town with very little diversity. By the time I reached middle school, I had still never met someone from outside the US. This changed when my school got its first foreign exchange student. She was from Thailand. I sat with her everyday at lunch and asked her countless questions about life in Thailand. This was my introduction to human diversity and it continued throughout university as I applied to live in the international dormitory and was suddenly introduced to people from all over the world. I quickly befriended them and began learning about their culture. Throughout my four years, I made an effort to learn about different kinds of people and to appreciate their differences. I have continued to do this in every phase of my life by observing and comparing cultures and I have made it my personal mission to promote tolerance and diversity wherever I am.
I am certain that by earning my Master of Arts in Social Anthropology, I can bring my past experiences into alignment and focus on my recently unearthed core values. I am undoubtedly an observer of people and this program will allow me to distill empirical conclusions from my observations. Through my studies in this program and eventually in my future career, I would like to bridge global cultural divisions, promote tolerance of others, and resolve international conflict. I would like to do this through international development and NGO work and this social anthropology program will provide me with the skills necessary to be successful.

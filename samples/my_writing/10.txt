eJournal 5: What Should We Believe?

Wag the Dog, released in 1997, is a very influential film depicting a public relations expert and a Hollywood producer distracting the public from a presidential scandal by creating a war. The film raises several very important questions about media and politics and persuades its viewers to examine the media they are being fed for inaccuracies. The film makes the viewer question the war in Iraq and the events surrounding it. In fact, the movie forces its viewers to look at all media with an examining eye.
The Jessica Lynch story is a recent event that parallels the methods of persuasion and distraction used by the government and media. Jessica Lynch, as depicted by the media, had been captured by Iraqi military after the vehicle she was riding in was hit with a grenade. She underwent torture and harsh conditions until she was ‘rescued’ by American military who were forced to use weapons when taking her from the hospital. According to Lynch, however, the reality behind this narrative was much different. She was indeed taken to an Iraqi hospital, but she was treated appropriately and the hospital workers were very kind to her, even trying to return her to the Americans (Lynch, 2007). Another article even suggests that her ‘rescue’ was staged as Iraqi militants had fled the hospital a day before. The Americans used blanks to create the effect that her rescue was dangerous and that a battle ensued.
Obviously, this event showed that the US government and military were trying to feed the American public a narrative--a narrative that would distract people from the war that was going on the reason that we were at war. Indeed the government did distract the American public, at least until Lynch attempted to set the record straight. Many people--some of my family members included--still believe the original media claims and do not even realize that Jessica Lynch spoke out against the media and the military press. Thus, something more must be done to illuminate the ability of the media and government to distract and persuade. Many people believe that the Internet will help in educated people about investigating the narratives that they are being given. The blogosphere was the first place to question the multibillion dollar bailout plan introduced by the government, providing proof that the Internet will lead in providing counter-narratives to the leading narratives introduced by the mainstream media and government.
After all, reality can only be depicted by clearing the fog of the narrative put in place by our media and government systems.
